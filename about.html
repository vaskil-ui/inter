Our story

A converted machine shop, one press, and no interest in shortcuts.

2014 Founded in a rented corner of a bike-repair shop
1904 Year our Vandercook press was built
400+ Suites and editions printed to date

Almanac started when our founder, a graphic designer tired of watching her work disappear into a screen, bought a broken-down letterpress at an estate sale and spent a winter learning to fix it. The first jobs were favors for friends: a wedding invitation here, a set of business cards there.

A decade on, the press still lives in that same neighborhood, now in a proper studio with room for a paper cutter, a type cabinet rescued from a closing newspaper, and drawers of ink mixed by hand to match colors that don't exist in any swatch book.

We still take on every project the same way: a conversation first, then sketches, then a proof you can hold before we commit it to a full run. Nothing leaves the shop until it's been checked by hand, sheet by sheet.

What we hold onto

Three things guide every job that comes through the studio, from a single business card to a five-hundred-piece wedding suite.

Small batches

We print in runs that suit the job, not the press. Nothing sits in a warehouse waiting for a buyer.

Traceable materials

Our cotton papers and soy-based inks come from mills and suppliers we've visited ourselves.

Work you can feel

Every plate is set deep enough that you can read a piece with your fingertips before you read it with your eyes.
